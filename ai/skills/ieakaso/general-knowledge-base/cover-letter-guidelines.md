# Cover letter guidelines

What makes a cover letter land an interview. The Applicant Agent writes by these guidelines, and the Seasoned Recruiter reviews against them. The rules for facts, layout, language and the 350-word cap live in `ai/agents/applicant.md`. Those rules win wherever this file seems to disagree.

Condensed from practitioner advice (sources at the end). It is opinion, not research. The research behind it, with data on whether letters work, is `cover-letters-tech-guide-2026-10-15.md`; it is for maintainers, and the agents do not read it.

---

## The test

Read the draft as the hiring manager for this role: **would this letter make me want to meet the candidate?** If not, it is not done. Every rule below serves this test.

A good letter is a clear, honest conversation with the hiring manager. **Clarity beats cleverness.**

---

## Structure: hook, book, took

Three moves, in this order, one short paragraph each (a fourth only if a gap needs addressing):

1. **Hook**: who the candidate is and the strongest reason to read on, aimed at what this role needs. The busy reader may stop after this paragraph, so put the most important information first: the position, the candidate's identity in one line, and the two or three strengths that matter most to this ad.
2. **Book**: the argument for hiring. Two or three of the ad's needs, each answered with **evidence** from the Personal knowledge base: a specific situation, what they did, the result (with a number when the Personal knowledge base has one), and why it is relevant to this role. That last step is the R in STARR, and it is the one most often missing.
3. **Took**: what the candidate wants to happen next. A concrete, confident close that invites a conversation.

## Rules

### About them, not the candidate

- Frame every point as the answer to the company's problem. "Your ad mentions streamlining workflows; at X I cut meeting time by 20% by…" beats "I am skilled at streamlining workflows".
- **Pain points:** in the ad, the "what you'll do" or "responsibilities" section says what hurts, more than the requirements list does. Pick the **top three tasks** there. They are what the Book answers.
- Ask what core problem this role exists to solve, and make sure the letter answers it.

### Speak their dialect

- Use the ad's own terms for tools, methods and outcomes ("CRM", not "customer database"; their name for the role, the team, the product). This is clarity, and it also helps any screening software.
- Mirror the ad's register: a startup ad written in "you" and first names gets a warmer letter than a corporate ad.

### Why this company: real or nothing

- The reason must be specific to this company and true for the candidate: a product, a mission, a challenge or a way of working named in the Job ad or the Company research, tied to a motive from the Personal knowledge base. "Your team is rebuilding the payments platform; that migration is the work I've done for the last four years" is real.
- If the Personal knowledge base gives no genuine motive that connects to this company, focus on the role itself (the problem it solves and why the candidate wants that problem) rather than inventing admiration.

### Evidence, not adjectives

- Every claimed strength comes with a short story that proves it. "Excellent communicator" is empty. The time the candidate aligned three teams on a migration plan is evidence.
- Turn CV bullets into full sentences with context. The letter adds the *why* and the *so what* that the CV cannot hold. It never re-lists the CV.
- **Paragraphs, not bullets.** The body is short paragraphs only: no bullet lists, no bold. Evidence goes in sentences.

### Reference letters

- A reference letter in the Personal knowledge base is trusted background. Its facts about the candidate (a team they led, a project they shipped) may be used as the candidate's own claims.
- Its opinions ("the best manager I've had") never appear in any form. They may only steer what the letter stresses.
- Never quote a reference letter, name its author, or attribute anything to it ("my former CTO described me as…").

### Emphasis by role

Work out the role function from the Job ad, then stress what readers of that function look for. This changes the emphasis, never the layout.

- **Software Engineer, Senior or Staff**: one specific system the candidate shipped, its scale, and *their own* contribution. At Staff and above, add direction (architecture, design docs that moved several teams), influence (mentoring, building capacity) and external standing (talks, open source, writing); without them a letter reads as senior, whatever the title. Be plainer and more personal for small startups; lead with numbers for scale-ups and large companies.
- **Engineering Manager**: people first, technical credibility second, as the interview loop does. Team scope (headcount, budget), team building (hires, retention while scaling), team health (engagement, reports promoted), delivery (lead time, predictability), and one turnaround or crisis story.
- **Product Manager**: one outcome metric (activation, retention, revenue) with its size and timeline in the first two sentences, tied to the company's product problem. Senior PMs open with a short thesis on the product or market. PM is a writing role, so the letter is also a writing sample.
- **Head of Engineering, Director or VP**: business outcomes over implementation (revenue, cost, reliability, time to market), the size of the organisation led, and experience with the company's stage (e.g. scaling from 20 to 80 engineers, or a platform rebuild). Motivation matters most at this level: say why this company, now. These roles are often filled through a short positioning note instead of a letter; Ieakaso writes only the A4 letter, so make these points there.

### Gaps, honestly

- A missing requirement does not have to be hidden or apologised for. If it matters, name what the candidate has done that comes closest (adjacent experience, a project, recent learning) and keep it to one or two sentences.
- Show understanding of the job. A letter that shows the candidate understands the work can outweigh a missing year of experience.

### Voice

- Write as the candidate speaks when being polite: informal but not casual, enthusiastic without hype.
- **Read it aloud.** Any sentence that sounds stiff, robotic or like a template gets rewritten in the candidate's natural voice (`input/writing-style.md`).
- **No AI tells.** Readers now spot machine-written letters, and generic content gives them away more than any single word. Avoid the words "delve", "pivotal" and "underscore", the "not just X, but Y" construction, reflexive lists of three ("strategy, execution and people"), and bold or other markup in the body.

### Formal-letter markets

- Germany (and the rest of DACH), France and the Netherlands still expect a formal letter, read with care. When the letter is in the local language, or the company is a traditional employer there, write a fuller, more formal and fact-based letter: less storytelling, no casual phrasing, the formal salutation and sign-off.
- English-first scale-ups in those countries take the usual direct letter.
- Formality never lifts the 350-word cap (`ai/agents/applicant.md`).

### Logistics line

- One sentence on time zone, work authorization, relocation or start date, **only when the Job ad states such a requirement and the Personal knowledge base answers it** (e.g. `config.yml`'s time zone or visa status). For example: "I'm based in Berlin and need no visa sponsorship."
- Otherwise leave logistics out. Never guess an answer, and never raise a requirement the ad does not state.
- It goes near the close, never in the Hook.

### Length

- Target **200 to 300 words** in the body. 350 is the hard cap (`ai/agents/applicant.md`), not the goal.
- Shorter is better when the evidence is strong. A tight 150-word letter with two sharp proofs beats a padded 320-word one.
- Between 300 and 350 words, every paragraph must earn its place.

### Salutation and sign-off

- Use the hiring manager's name when the Job ad or the Personal knowledge base gives it.
- Otherwise use the letter language's standard formal salutation for an unnamed reader: "Dear Hiring Team" in English, "Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren" in German.
- Use a courtesy title (Mr, Ms, Dr) only where the letter's language expects one, as German does with "Sehr geehrte Frau …". In English, use first and last name.
- The sign-off matches the salutation's register and the letter's language. "Yours faithfully" is only for an unnamed recipient in formal British English. "Best regards" or "Kind regards" fits most English letters.

## Anti-patterns

Each of these marks a letter as generic. The Recruiter flags them; the Applicant rewrites them into the positive form above.

| Anti-pattern | Example | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Empty company praise | "Your company is a leader in the industry." / "Bosch's reputation as a global leader…strongly resonates with my professional values." | One specific, true reason tied to the candidate's own motive |
| Formula opening | "I am writing to express my interest in…" | Hook: the strongest match to the role, first sentence |
| Adjectives as evidence | "my analytical mindset, attention to detail, and strategic thinking" | One short story with a result |
| CV re-listed | A bullet list of duties that matches the CV | Two or three needs mapped to evidence, with context |
| Claims with nothing behind them | "Extensive project management experience", "proven leadership" | Name the project, the team size, the outcome |
| Unfilled placeholders | "[highlight a specific achievement]" | A real achievement from the Personal knowledge base, or cut the sentence |
| Generic close | "I look forward to the possibility of contributing to X's continued success." | A concrete next step |
| AI-flavoured filler | "passion for innovation and excellence", "valuable addition to your team", "drive continued success" | Plain words the candidate would say aloud |
| AI tells | "delve", "pivotal", "underscore", "not just X, but Y", "strategy, execution and people", bold in the body | Plain sentences; one example instead of three |
| Bullet list in the body | "I've previously:" followed by bullets | The same evidence in one or two sentences |
| Quoted or attributed reference letter | "My former CTO called me an exceptional leader." | Cut it. A fact from the reference letter may go in as the candidate's own claim; an opinion only steers emphasis |

### The generic letter, whole

This letter could go to any company for any controller role. Most of the anti-patterns above are in it. If a draft reads like it, start again.

> Dear Hiring Manager,
>
> I am writing to express my interest in the Controller position at Bosch. With my strong background in financial management and accounting, combined with my passion for innovation and excellence, I believe I would be a valuable addition to your financial leadership team.
>
> Bosch's reputation as a global leader in technology and services, with a commitment to sustainability and quality, strongly resonates with my professional values. I am particularly impressed by Bosch's dedication to innovation across diverse sectors while maintaining financial discipline and integrity.
>
> Throughout my career, I have:
>
> - Developed and implemented robust financial controls and reporting systems
> - Led financial planning and analysis initiatives that supported strategic decision-making
> - Managed cross-functional teams to improve financial processes and operational efficiency
> - Ensured compliance with accounting standards and regulatory requirements
>
> In my current role as [Your Current Position] at [Your Current Company], I have successfully [highlight a specific achievement relevant to the controller position]. This experience has prepared me to take on the challenges and responsibilities of a Controller at Bosch.
>
> I am confident that my analytical mindset, attention to detail, and strategic thinking would allow me to make significant contributions to Bosch's financial management and overall business objectives. I am excited about the opportunity to bring my skills and experience to your organization and help drive continued financial success.
>
> I welcome the opportunity to discuss how my qualifications align with your needs. Thank you for considering my application, and I look forward to the possibility of contributing to Bosch's continued success.
>
> Sincerely,
>
> [Your Name]

### A short letter that works

Specific reason, evidence with numbers, a warm close: 90 words. Its bullets suit an email; in Ieakaso's A4 letter the same evidence goes in sentences.

> Dear Igor,
>
> I'd love to work with you on the position of Growth Marketer. I've been following Handpicked Berlin since 2023 and I really like the way it has developed (I'm a big fan of the "Meanwhile" section and the articles on German!).
>
> I think I can help you grow the social media accounts and the newsletter even more. I've previously:
>
> - Built a TikTok strategy from scratch for [Brand] that reached 45K followers in 3 months with Berlin-specific travel content
> - Implemented a multi-touch attribution model that helped grow the newsletter by 120% in 6 months
>
> The rest is in my CV. Can't wait to talk to you!
